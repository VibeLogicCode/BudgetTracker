# v1.8.0 — Account Balances and Backlog Cleanup

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development to
> implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Give every account a balance history, and clear the eight remaining backlog items
from `docs/PENDING-FIXES.md` and the v1.5.0 ledger.

**Architecture:** `account_balance_snapshots` already exists (migration 0009) with two
writers: SimpleFIN (`src/lib/simplefin/sync.ts:200`) and a manual "balance as of date" form
on Settings → Accounts (`src/app/(app)/settings/accounts/actions.ts:202`). This release adds
the third writer — a statement's own running-balance column, where the bank provides one —
and a resolver that answers "what did this account hold on date D" as *the newest snapshot at
or before D, plus the signed sum of transactions after it*. A statement CSV is a complete
ledger of an account (every charge, payment, fee and interest line is a row), so that sum is
exact rather than approximate, and entering a fresh snapshot at any date re-anchors
everything after it.

**Tech Stack:** Next.js 15 App Router, TypeScript, SQLite (better-sqlite3 + Drizzle),
Vitest, zod.

**Spec:** this document (spec and plan combined, same convention as
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-22-v1.7.0-finish-line-design.md`).

## What this release does NOT build, and why

Recorded because two earlier drafts of this spec designed both of them before checking:

- **Anchor columns on `accounts`.** Not needed. The manual snapshot IS the anchor — the
  "balance as of date" form has shipped since v1.7.0 and writes `source='manual'` with an
  as-of date.
- **Backward reconstruction from an anchor** (`deriveBalances`, a contiguity/gap rule,
  `MAX_DERIVE_GAP_DAYS`, stored `source='derived'` rows). Withdrawn. A forward sum from the
  newest snapshot answers the same question, is self-correcting rather than accumulating
  drift, handles out-of-order imports for free (it is a `WHERE date >` comparison, not an
  increment), and is a fraction of the code.
- **A denormalized `accounts.balance_cents` field.** Withdrawn. A cached running total only
  ever incremented can never self-correct, and double-counts when an older statement is
  imported after a newer one. This repo has already been bitten five times by stale cached
  aggregates (see the v1.7.0 alert-fingerprint defects). Compute, do not cache.

## Global Constraints

- **Version:** `1.8.0`. `package.json`, `CHANGELOG.md`, and the `MUST-7.1` test in

## Design rulings

Decisions, not options. An implementer who disagrees raises it rather than diverging.

**R1 — The balance resolver reads RAW transaction amounts.** `balanceAsOf` sums
`transactions.amount_cents` off the parent table with no `is_transfer` filter, no
`transaction_splits` join, and no reuse of any spend-aggregate helper. **This is the single
most dangerous line in the release.** A credit-card payment is normally marked
`is_transfer=1` to keep it out of spend reporting; inherit that filter here and every card
payment disappears from the balance, so the card balance climbs forever while looking
entirely plausible. Splits are the same trap from the other direction — the parent's
`amount_cents` is the true movement, and `EFFECTIVE_AMOUNT`'s `coalesce` over a `LEFT JOIN`
must not appear anywhere in this path. Pin both with tests (Task 4, Steps 1 and 5).

**R2 — Newest snapshot at or before the date wins, then sum forward.** Never sum from the
oldest snapshot, and never sum across a newer snapshot that sits between it and the target
date. A snapshot is a hard re-anchor: it makes every balance after it correct regardless of
what came before, which is what makes drift self-healing and is the whole reason nothing is
cached.

**R3 — The bank's own figure outranks a typed one for the same date.** Source authority,
highest first: `simplefin`, `csv`, `manual`. All three keep today's last-write-wins upsert on
`(account_id, date)`. `'csv'` is a new source value precisely so this ordering is expressible;
writing statement balances as `'manual'` would make a statement figure and a hand-typed guess
indistinguishable.

**R4 — Same-date rows take the last balance in chronological order.** A statement can carry
several rows for one day (the owner's TD export has three on one date). The day's closing
balance is the balance on the last of them *in file order once direction is known* — not the
max, not the min, not the first.

**R5 — File direction is detected, never assumed.** Compare the first and last successfully
parsed dates. Oldest-first means the last row of a date group carries that date's closing
balance; newest-first means the first row does. Assuming one direction silently inverts every
balance for banks exporting the other way. A single-date file is treated as oldest-first.

**R6 — A malformed balance cell is not a row error.** A transaction with an unparseable
balance is still a valid transaction. `balanceCents` becomes `null` for that row, no
`RowError` is produced, and the row still imports. Only that date's snapshot is skipped.

**R7 — Reconciliation reports, it never corrects.** A gap between two consecutive statement
balances and the transactions between them is surfaced with its amount and its date. Never
silently adjust a transaction, and never insert a plug entry.

**R8 — The Coming up card names both windows rather than reconciling them.** The 30-day list
and the month-end safe-to-spend figure are genuinely different questions and both are worth
showing. The defect is that the footer stated one without saying which. Fix the copy, keep
both numbers.

**R9 — Credit anchors are entered as money owed.** The existing manual form takes a signed
amount via `parseAmountToCents`. For `accounts.type === 'credit'` the label becomes "Amount
currently owed" and the value is negated on write, so a card owing $500 stores `-50000`.
Accepting a raw signed number is how a $500 debt becomes a $500 asset and moves net worth by
$1,000 the wrong way. `chequing` / `cash` keep "Balance" and store the sign as typed. The
negation happens in the action, keeping `src/lib/networth.ts`'s "nothing in this file
normalizes a sign" rule true of the lib layer.

-- SQLite cannot ALTER a CHECK constraint, so the table is recreated. Constraint 26 in

CREATE TABLE `account_balance_snapshots` (

	`source` text NOT NULL,

only record of balances that no CSV can regenerate. Any later constraint change on it needs
the real rebuild: `__new_` table, `INSERT ... SELECT`, `DROP`, `RENAME`, re-`CREATE` the

Then `closingBalancesByDate`, whose only job is R4 + R5:

```ts
/**
 * That date's CLOSING balance per ruling R4: the balance on the last row of the date group in
 * chronological order. For an oldest-first file that is the last such row in file order; for
 * a newest-first file it is the first. Rows whose balance cell did not parse (ruling R6) are
 * skipped, and a date with no parseable balance at all gets no entry rather than a zero.
 */
export function closingBalancesByDate(
  rows: CandidateRow[],
  dateOrder: ParseResult['dateOrder'],
): Map<string, number> {
  const out = new Map<string, number>();
  const ordered = dateOrder === 'newest_first' ? [...rows].reverse() : rows;
  for (const row of ordered) {
    if (row.balanceCents === null) continue;
    out.set(row.date, row.balanceCents); // later write wins => last row of the group
  }
  return out;
}
```

```ts
/**
 * F5 fix (v1.8.0). One promise per src, module-level, so a SCANNER_LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS timeout
 * followed by a retry attaches to the still-in-flight injection instead of appending a second
 * <script> and standing up a second Emscripten runtime with its own ~9 MB wasm heap. A timeout
 * only loses loadScanner's Promise.race; it cancels nothing, so the first load is still
 * coming. A genuine onerror is different -- the script will never arrive -- so that case
 * DELETES the entry and lets the next call retry for real.
 */
const injections = new Map<string, Promise<void>>();

function injectScript(src: string): Promise<void> {
  const existing = injections.get(src);
  if (existing !== undefined) return existing;
  const promise = new Promise<void>((resolve, reject) => {
    const element = document.createElement('script');
    element.src = src;
    element.async = true;
    element.onload = () => resolve();
    element.onerror = () => {
      injections.delete(src);
      reject(new Error(`could not load ${src}`));
    };
    document.head.appendChild(element);
  });
  injections.set(src, promise);
  return promise;
}
```

## Deferred: "Money Lent" as a lending ledger

**Not in this release.** Recorded so the design survives.

The owner's proposal: when a shared bill is split, categorise the other person's share to a
"Money Lent" category and name the person, so it reads as an amount outstanding until they pay
it back. Better fit for the problem than the per-split attribution previously queued, and it
needs no new table.

**Two things it needs that a plain category does not give it:**

1. **`categories.excluded_from_spend`.** Until the category is excluded from spend aggregates,
   other people's share inflates the owner's own monthly spending — the exact problem the
   split was made to solve. There is precedent for the flag (`isIncome` on categories,
   `is_transfer` on transactions), but adding it means re-auditing every spend aggregate for
   the same double-counting class the splits review turned up five defects in. That audit is
   the real cost: ~3-4h, and it is a feature, not a fix.

2. **An outstanding-by-person view.** Every report in this app is month- or range-scoped, so
   lent in July and repaid in September shows `+$60` in July and `-$60` in September, and
   nothing anywhere states "Bob owes $60". The balance the owner wants does not exist until
   something sums the category all-time and groups it.

**Use the split's `note` for the person's name, never a description override.** Merchant rules
train on the description; rewriting it to a person's name teaches the categoriser that that
name means "restaurant", and the merchant name is lost from the transaction.

**Alternative considered and rejected for now:** model it as a loan receivable. The loans
subsystem already has an outstanding balance, correct spend exclusion, direction-aware
`applied_cents`, and `debtOverTime` — conceptually a much better fit. Rejected only because
`loan_payments` links to whole transactions, not to splits, and adding split-level linking is
more work than the category flag.
