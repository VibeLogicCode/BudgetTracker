## Design Specificity Verdict

**The token system is authored. The composition is not.**

Authored to this product: money typography (`.money-xl`, tabular figures, display cut, -0.03em); the fill-vs-text colour split (`--positive-solid` separate from `--positive`, because "holding a bar to the text ratio is what turns amber into mud"); `deltaTone` as a prop separate from `tone`, because spending up is bad news even when the number is unsigned; the honest-disclosure family ("(partial)" beside net worth, "as of today, not March"); the phone table-to-card reflow on one DOM; and the voice — "Runs on your own hardware. Nothing leaves the house."

Generic: the chrome. A 256px fixed rail, a 64px sticky blurred header, avatar-plus-chevron top right — the unmodified SaaS default. `--accent: #4b49d6` is the most-used dashboard indigo in existence, and nothing in "household ledger" argues for it. Every page is a stack of identical hairline cards.

**Verdict: the system has a point of view; the pages do not express it.** The app is not ugly and not broken — it is inoffensive, which is the exact failure being asked about. The authored parts are buried in a layout that gives them no stage.

## What's Working

**Money typography is a real decision, correctly scoped.** Figures get the display cut, tabular numerals, 650 weight; exactly one tile per row is promoted to `money-xl`. It works because financial scanning is two tasks — aligning a column and judging a magnitude — and tabular figures serve the first while the size step serves the second. Most systems apply one scale to both and fail both.

**The colour system encodes meaning the UI would otherwise get wrong.** The fill-vs-text split, `--placeholder` deliberately tuned *below* AA at ~3.3:1 so a hint never reads as a value, and `deltaTone` separate from `tone`. Each eliminates a bug class at the token layer instead of patching per component. Token discipline is total: zero Tailwind palette utilities, zero arbitrary colour values across 125 `.tsx` files.

**Empty and error states are written for a real person.** `error.tsx` detects that the user pressed "Update now" thirty seconds ago and rewrites "something went wrong" into "Installing v1.39.1 — this screen is that gap, not a fault." `EmptyState`'s `noAction` prop is a *typed* way to say "no honest action exists here."

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The dashboard has no hierarchy — 13 equal cards in one column

Everything from Getting started to the Goals grid renders as the same `.card`: same hairline, same radius, no shadow, same `CardHeader` weight. "7 transactions need review" and "a warranty expires in 83 days" carry identical visual weight. The shadow was removed deliberately and correctly — nine drop shadows per page *was* noise — but nothing replaced it, so the app lost its last ranking mechanism and gained nothing back.

**Why it matters:** this is the whole answer to "can it look nicer." Calm is not the problem; *flat* is. A person opens the app with one question — is this month okay? — and a scroll of identical rectangles refuses to answer it.

**Fix:** (1) Lift the stat grid out of card-land onto a `--surface-2` hero band with hairline dividers, so the `money-xl` figure sits on a ground rather than in a box. (2) Two columns at `lg` — left 2/3 for cards that ask for action, right 1/3 for reference. (3) Reintroduce one elevation step (`--elev-card` still exists) reserved for cards that are asking for something, two or three per page maximum.

**Command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P0] The nav rail is ten undifferentiated doors

`nav.ts` contains an excellent docblock explaining that the entries follow the order money moves through the app — see the month, check transactions, fix what was unsure, bring more in, then planning, then back office — and **none of that structure is visible in the rendering.** Ten flat `text-sm` rows, no grouping, no dividers, no section labels. "Contracts & Coverage" is the longest label and relies on `truncate` to survive.

**Why it matters:** a flat ten-item list is a lookup table; the docblock describes a *ritual*. Showing the sequence would make the rail teach the app. A label designed to be clipped is not a finished label.

**Fix:** three groups using the `.eyebrow` treatment already in the system — **This month** (Dashboard, Transactions, Review, Import) · **Plan** (Budgets, Goals, Coverage, Reports) · hairline, then Settings. Move Help out of the rail; it is already in the footer on every page. Rename to "Coverage". Narrow the rail to 224px.

**Command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] Category dropdowns are clipped inside dialogs — functional defect

`CategoryCombobox`'s listbox is `absolute z-20 max-h-64` (`CategoryCombobox.tsx:225`). All four call sites sit inside `RowDialog`, whose panel wraps `<Card as="div">` — and `Card.tsx:21` is `card overflow-hidden`, a hard clip. A category list opened low in a dialog is cut off by its own container.

This affects the split editor, the recategorize dialog, and the Create-a-rule dialog shipped in v1.39.0. `RowMenu` already solved this exact problem — it is `position: fixed` placed from `getBoundingClientRect()` specifically to escape `TableWrap`'s overflow, and its comment names the prior clipped-label defect. The combobox did not inherit the fix.

**Fix:** give the listbox the `RowMenu` treatment — fixed positioning from the input's rect — or portal it.

**Command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] The sticky header is 64px of duplicated content

At `lg` the hamburger and mobile logo are hidden, so the header's entire left side is one span carrying the current section's name — **the same word `PageHeader` renders as an `<h1>` about 28px below it.** The app states its location twice on every page, the smaller one first. That is the most expensive wasted space in the product, and it is at eye level.

**Fix:** either drop the span at `lg` and give the header something global (the month scope, so it stops being re-declared per page), or hoist the page title into the header and let pages start with content. The second removes a whole band from every page.

**Command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] `PageGuide` wears the costume of a system notice, on nine pages

The guide renders `bg-info-soft` with a bold summary — byte-for-byte the same treatment as a real message about the user's data ("Viewing March 2026. Net worth, loans… still reflect today"). It appears at the top of nine pages, closed by default, permanently.

**Why it matters:** two failures at once. Info-blue stops meaning "something is true about your data" and starts meaning wallpaper, so the genuine banner gets skipped. And the guide copy — some of the best writing in the product — is dressed as the thing users are trained to dismiss.

**Fix:** demote the guide to a quiet `<details>` on `--surface` with a hairline, or move it into `PageHeader` as an "About this page" link. Reserve `--info-soft` exclusively for statements about the user's data.

**Command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Two badge systems and an inverted heading ladder

`.badge--*` (hue-named, `px-2 py-0.5`, weight 550) and `<Pill tone="…">` (semantic, `px-2.5 py-1`, `font-medium`) both render on the dashboard at once — the nav review count is a badge, NeedsALookCard's kind label is a Pill. Two chips, two sizes, two colour vocabularies, one screen.

Separately: `SectionHeader` uses `.eyebrow` (11px uppercase `--subtle`) while `CardHeader` uses 16px semibold `--ink`. So "Goals", which introduces an entire grid, is visually *subordinate* to "Top merchants", which titles one card. The ladder runs backwards.

**Fix:** make `.badge` the visual primitive and have `Pill` compose it through a semantic-to-hue map. Give `SectionHeader` its own step *above* `CardHeader` — `text-lg font-semibold text-ink` — and reserve the eyebrow for `StatTile` labels, the job it was invented for.

**Command:** `$impeccable typeset`

## Cognitive Load

**Decisions with more than 4 visible options:** the dashboard header puts nine interactive elements above the first number in a three-person household (QuickAdd + SendDigest + MonthNav's three targets + one pill per person). The transactions row has eight columns, three interactive, plus a kebab holding seven actions — so the primary decision, *what is this charge?*, is one of seven behind `⋯`. Budgets stacks four buttons in one `CardHeader` action slot, repeated per person. Reports has five controls silently driving eleven cards.

**State held in the head:** which cards follow the month and which say "today". The dashboard's answer is to print the same grey sentence at five separate places down one column, plus a page-level banner. Six restatements of one rule is an admission the layout never solved it — group the today-scoped cards under one heading instead.

**Density:** first run shows five empty states at once, four of them offering the identical "Import a statement" button.

## Emotional Journey

**After importing a statement** — the highest-effort act in the app — you get up to three stacked banners and a link to a review queue. The end feeling is "here is more homework," not "done." Nothing says what your month now looks like.

**After categorising a pile** — the app's best moment, and nearly right: a check icon, "Nothing to review. Everything is categorized," two forward doors. Undercut by one thing: the progress bar above it is session-local, so a refresh erases the evidence of forty minutes of work.

**On a bad-money day** — six sources of amber and red visible at once, each formally identical, none subordinated, nothing calm to land on. The app handles bad news with grace in exactly one place: NeedsALookCard's *"Nothing here is a problem on its own."* That is a designer thinking about a person's stomach, and it is the only instance. Nothing anywhere says "one month is not a trend."

## Persona Red Flags

**The owner, in the app daily.** Every per-row action is behind `⋯` — on the workflow the codebase itself names ("auditing fifty rows after an import"), that is fifty menu opens. Account/person/date filters are collapsed behind a 32px icon whose only visible state is a count badge, re-opened every single day. Reports costs a full page load per thought. Review progress evaporates on refresh.

**The household member who only reviews their own spending.** `visibleNav` strips Import/Review/Settings and `selfScoped` removes the person pills, net worth, loans and top merchants — what remains is two or three tiles and a lot of gap. Not a focused page, an emptied one; nothing rebalances the grid to compensate. Their page guide is one sentence where a household viewer gets three paragraphs — the person with the least context gets the least explanation.

**The first-timer, empty database.** Five empty states on the first screen, four repeating the same instruction. The login screen buries its best line: *"Runs on your own hardware. Nothing leaves the house."* — the one sentence that distinguishes this product — at `text-xs text-subtle`, the smallest and faintest text on the page.

## Questions to Consider

1. If money is meant to be the loudest thing on the page, why is the loudest number in a box identical to the one holding "Top merchants"? What does the top of the dashboard look like if it is not cards at all?
2. `nav.ts` describes a monthly ritual in detail and the rail shows none of it. What if the rail *were* the ritual — would the app then teach a new household member what to do without them opening Help?
3. Every explanatory sentence in this product is excellent, and every one is collapsed, muted, or in a blue box. What happens if the sentence is set at 24px and the figure supports it, rather than the reverse? This app's differentiator is that it talks like a person. It currently whispers.
4. On the worst money day of the month the app goes red in six places and says nothing. What is the one sentence it should say then, and where would it go?
